Methodology & dataset overview

Format: Instagram “Download your information” HTML export (12 files, message_1…12.html, newest first; re-sorted chronologically). Each entry has a sender, a minute-resolution timestamp, body text and optional media/reaction tags.

Sender mapping (confirmed from the file header “Generated by _rahul_pun”): PUN = the exporting account (you); kritika. = your girlfriend. Please correct me if this is reversed.

Definitions: “message” = text, voice note, photo, video, shared post/reel, GIF or unclassified entry. Reaction events and call logs are excluded from volume, response and initiation metrics. A session starts after a 120-minute silence; the initiator is the sender of its first message; a reply is a sender change inside a session. Language metrics use text messages only (URLs and “(edited)” markers removed). Affection, humour, support and friction figures are keyword/emoji markers (English + romanised Nepali), reported per 1,000 texts.

Parsing limitations

  • The request window was 22 Nov 2025–22 Nov 2026 but the first message is 29 Jan 2026; I cannot tell whether earlier history existed.
  • Timestamps have one-minute resolution and no stated timezone (shown as exported). “0 min” replies mean “within the same minute”.
  • “Reacted … to your message” events are logged only for Kritika (6,271; PUN: 0) — likely an export artefact, so they are excluded from comparisons. Inline reactions (both directions) are used instead.
  • 1,120 entries have no recoverable content (possibly stickers or unsupported items) and are counted only as messages. “(edited)” appears on 772 of PUN’s messages and none of Kritika’s; this may be an export quirk.
  • Shared reels/posts carry third-party captions, which are excluded from language analysis.
  • Heavy romanised-Nepali/English mixing, inconsistent spelling and humour make dictionary sentiment unreliable; all marker counts are lower bounds and are not sentiment scores.
  • Only this Instagram thread is covered: other channels, calls’ content and in-person time are invisible.

Strengths & possible areas to explore

Strengths (measured)

  • Near-even participation and rapid mutual replies.
  • Contact on almost every day, including a 186-day streak.
  • Shared humour and frequent warm reactions in both directions.
  • Mutual apology language and quick return to affection markers after flagged friction.
  • Daily greeting/goodnight rituals from both.

Areas to explore (neutral)

  • Explicit affection and check-in phrasing differ in frequency; you could ask each other which forms of care feel most meaningful.
  • Kritika opens more sessions while PUN closes more; notice whether both feel comfortable with that rhythm.
  • Support wording (check-ins, reassurance) was below 5 per 1,000 texts for both; deeper talks may happen by voice/call, which the export cannot show.
  • Review the flagged friction dates together if useful; the tool cannot judge them.

Limitations & conclusion

This report describes one Instagram thread over about ten months. Counts, speeds and markers are not measures of love, loyalty, commitment or relationship quality, and nothing here diagnoses either person or infers private intentions. Marker lexicons are approximate and lean towards under-counting.

Conclusion. The record shows a high-frequency, balanced, quick-reply and humour-rich conversation that grew busier and more affection-marked over time, with complementary styles. The most useful next step is to treat the patterns as conversation starters rather than verdicts.
